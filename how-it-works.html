Setup

Seven steps, and two of them are optional.

These times are for a small menu and someone who has never done this before. Nothing is charged at any point, and you can stop halfway and come back later.

01
1 minute

Create the account

Your restaurant name, your email, and a password. No card, and nothing on this page costs you anything.

  • You pick your web address here, and that is the link you hand out
  • Then a short setup guide walks you through the rest of this list
02
5 to 30 minutes

Get your menu in

Four ways to do it, and you can mix them. Type your food in, upload a spreadsheet, paste a link to a menu you already have online and check what we pulled out of it, or send it to us and we type it in for you, free.

  • Nothing is saved until you have looked at the list yourself and pressed Import
  • You can change prices, sections, options and photos any time afterwards
  • Start with your ten best sellers if you want to be open today, and finish later
03
3 minutes

Set your hours and pickup times

When you take orders, and how long a customer should expect to wait. Your page opens and closes on that schedule by itself, so nobody pays for food at 2am.

  • Different hours for different days, and close for a holiday without deleting anything
  • You have to set these before you can open, and clearing them later leaves you open rather than quietly going dark
04
1 minute

Say how orders reach your kitchen

A tablet by the pass, a kitchen printer, both, or email on its own. Email is in every one of them, because paper jams and an inbox has no moving parts. You cannot open without choosing, since a ticket nobody sees is a customer waiting for food nobody is making.

  • Your own login always gets a copy, and you can add up to eight more inboxes
  • If you pick a printer, it has to print a test ticket before you can open - pick email only if yours has not arrived yet
05
5 to 10 minutes

Connect payments

Card payments run through your own Stripe account. The money goes to your bank the way it already does, and never passes through us.

  • Never used Stripe? Signing up is part of the setup guide and takes about ten minutes
  • Already on Stripe? Connect the account you have and nothing about your payouts changes
  • Every order on your page is paid by card at checkout, so you cannot open until this is done
06
5 minutes

Make it look like you

Your logo, a big photo, and a color. You can skip this. A plain page taking orders beats a beautiful one that is still unfinished on Friday.

  • You see the real page change as you edit it, not a pretend version
  • You can use your own web address later, and then that is all your customers see
07
10 minutes

Put the link where your customers already are

This is the step that decides whether any of the others were worth doing. Your Google listing, your Instagram bio, and a QR code on the table and the counter.

  • Google puts an Order Online button on your listing once you add the link
  • We make the QR code for you, ready to print
The first order

What happens when an order comes in.

Five things happen, start to finish. You only have to do two of them.

  1. The customer orders and pays

    On their phone, on your page. Their card is charged at checkout through your own Stripe account, so the money is in before your kitchen does anything.

  2. The order lands on your screen

    The order appears on your screen with the food, any options, their notes, the pickup time and the total. We recommend that screen is a tablet on a stand by the pass - a spare one you already have, or about $75 from Amazon. It can be the phone in your pocket instead, which costs nothing at all. It can print in the kitchen too, if you buy a printer.

  3. You accept it and cook

    Accepting confirms the pickup time. If you have run out of something you can mark it unavailable and refund just that item, leaving the rest of the order alone.

  4. You mark it ready

    Your customer gets a message and their tracking page updates. They already have the link, so they are not stood at your counter asking if it is ready.

  5. They collect, and they are on your list

    Their name and number are now on your customer list. That is the part a delivery app keeps for itself.

Before you start

What to have to hand.

Four things, and you almost certainly have all four already.

Your menu

A photo of it is enough. Typing it in is quicker if it is short.

A bank account

In the restaurant's name, connected through Stripe. Same as any card machine.

Your opening hours

Just for the days you want online orders. It does not have to be every day.

About half an hour, once

You can do it in bits. Nothing has to be finished in one sitting.

Your screen

Run orders on a tablet by the pass, or on the phone in your pocket.

Ask us what setup to run and the answer is always the same: a screen by the pass showing nothing but orders, with email arriving underneath it as the backup. If you already have a spare tablet or an old iPad, that is the setup - it costs you nothing and it works today.

Straight about it

What we do for you, and what we do not do at all.
