Three ways it pays

You keep the money, the customers, and your freedom.

You keep what you made

No monthly bill, no cut of sales

A cut is taken off the whole order, not off your profit. If you make 10 cents of profit on a dollar, a 20 cent cut does not take a fifth of that order. It takes twice what you made on it. Read that twice, because it is the whole point.

The customer becomes yours

Their name and number, every order

A delivery app rents you one order and keeps the customer. That same person ordering straight from you next month costs you nothing. That list is what you are really building here.

There is no rent to pay

Nothing to pay back each month

A monthly bill has to be paid before it makes you a penny, in a slow January and in the week the boiler breaks. A free page is still worth having in a bad month. That is exactly when a paid one gets canceled.

The part nobody prices

The list is the thing you are actually building.

Saving that cut is worth having. A list of everybody who has ever ordered from you is worth more, and it is the one thing a delivery app will never give you.

The other side

Four things this will not do for you.

A page of nothing but good news is a page nobody believes. Here are the four catches, before you have to ask.

You still pay the card fee

About 2.9 percent plus 30 cents, paid to Stripe, the same as you already pay on your card machine. We do not add to it and it does not come through us.

This does not replace the delivery apps

Delivery apps are good at finding you new people. This is for the customers who already know you. Most restaurants use both and move the regulars over.

A page nobody visits earns nothing

The one thing that decides whether this works is putting your link on Google and a QR code on the table. It takes ten minutes, and skipping it means nothing else matters.

The customer fee is real, and we show it

About $1 to $2 on a normal order, shown on the receipt before your customer pays. We would rather you read it here than find it yourself later.
