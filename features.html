01

Your ordering page

The page your customers actually order from, and what it does for them.

A page of your own

Your name, your logo, your food, your prices, and nobody else's restaurant on it.

Customers open a link and order. There is no app for them to download and no account they have to make. It loads fast on a phone, which is where nearly all of it happens, and it can be saved to a home screen like an app if they want.

Five looks, and your own color

Pick a style, set your accent color, and the whole page follows it.

You are choosing a look, not building a website. The layout is fixed on purpose - it is the one we know converts - so there is no way to accidentally end up with a page that does not work on a phone. One of the five follows the customer's own light or dark setting.

See changes before anyone else does

The editor shows your real page updating as you type.

It is not a mock-up that might look different once saved: it is your actual storefront, previewing edits you have not committed. Nothing is public until you save, and customers cannot reach a preview.

Your own web address

Point a domain you own at your page and that is the only address customers see.

Once it is verified, every link we make uses your address - order tracking pages, receipts, QR codes, all of it. We handle the certificate, and an apex domain gets its www twin set up alongside it so nobody hits a security warning.

A QR code and a link, ready to use

For the counter, the window, the table, your Instagram bio and your Google listing.

Generated for you, no design work needed. The Google listing is the one that matters most: putting your link there is worth more to your order count than everything else put together, and we walk you through it.

02

Your menu

Getting your food into the system, and keeping it right during service.

Four ways to get your menu in

Type it, upload a spreadsheet, paste a link to a menu already online, or send it to us.

The link importer reads a menu you already have on a delivery platform and shows you what it found. Nothing is saved until you have looked at it, because a machine cannot reliably tell $12.00 from $1,200.00 or a dish from a topping. If you would rather not do any of it, send us what you have and we build it, free.

Sections, options and photos

Sizes, extras, choices, and a picture on anything you want.

As many items as you like, in the order you like. Change a price and the page updates straight away - there is no publish step to forget.

Sold out in one tap

Mark an item unavailable mid-service and it comes off the page immediately.

If somebody has already ordered it, you can mark just that item unavailable on their order and refund that line, leaving the rest of what they ordered alone. They get told automatically.

Hours that close ordering for you

Set the days and times you are open and the page switches itself off outside them.

Judged in your own timezone. You can also close ordering right now without deleting anything, and set how long pickup takes, so your page never promises fifteen minutes while the kitchen is running forty.

03

Taking orders

What happens between a customer paying and them walking out with food.

A live order board

New orders appear on their own, with a sound, without anyone refreshing.

It shows the food, the options, the customer's note, the pickup time and the total. You accept, cook, and mark it ready. It is a web page, so it runs on the laptop, phone or tablet you already own.

Four ways to be told, and email is in all of them

A tablet by the pass, a kitchen printer, both, or email on its own.

There is deliberately no printer-only option. Paper jams, runs out and gets unplugged, and an inbox has no moving parts. Your own login address is always on the recipient list and cannot be removed, and you can add up to eight other inboxes.

Kitchen printing

Tickets print in the kitchen the second an order is paid for.

The printer talks to us over your internet on its own - no computer or tablet in between and nothing to install. Tickets carry a rolling number so your line can call them out. If the printer stops answering, orders keep arriving by email and your dashboard tells you it is offline.

A tracking page for every customer

They get a link showing where their order is, so nobody stands at your counter asking.

No login needed - the link is the key. It shows your address, your phone number as digits they can read, and directions if we know where you are.

Refunds and cancellations

Refund a whole order or a single item off it, in a couple of taps.

Every change is recorded with a time and a reason, so a dispute weeks later has an actual timeline behind it rather than somebody's memory. Refunds are capped at what was paid, and a failed one is shown to you rather than swallowed.

Order history you can search

Every order you have ever taken, searchable, with what happened to it.

Including what was printed and when. When a customer says they ordered something they did not, this is the answer.

04

Money

Who pays what, where it goes, and whose bank account it lands in.

No monthly bill and no commission

We take no percentage of your sales and send you no invoice.

A $500 day and a $50 day cost you the same: nothing. A small service fee is added to the customer's ticket at checkout instead, shown to them as its own line before they pay. That is what funds the platform.

The money is yours from the moment it is paid

Card payments go to your own account and into your own bank.

We never hold your takings. Payments run through Stripe on an account in your name, and payouts follow Stripe's schedule straight to your bank. Nobody here can sit on your money, including us.

The fee is disclosed, always

Customers see it before they pay and again on the receipt.

It is around $1 to $2 on a normal order and only scales up on unusually large ones. It is generated as its own line and cannot be edited out of a receipt by anyone, including us.

05

Your customer list

The part that is actually the point: an asset you own and can take with you.

A name and number from every order

Built automatically as you trade, with what each person has ordered before.

This is the thing a delivery app never gives you. It is the difference between renting access to your own customers and owning the relationship.

Download it whenever you want, including on the way out

Export the lot to a spreadsheet, any time, no questions.

Holding your list hostage would defeat the entire point of the product, so there is nothing to ask for and nobody to ring.

Bring a list you already have

Upload a spreadsheet of customers and it merges with what you have.

It shows you what it will do before it does it, and an import can be undone - anyone who has since ordered is kept, so undoing never puts a hole in your own order history.

Tags, filters and saved groups

Group your regulars, your lunch trade, whoever you like.

Search by what they spent, when they last came in, how often, or a tag you made up. Save a search and it stays there.

Email your own customers

Your list, your message, sent by you.

We only ever email your customers about their own order - the confirmation, the ready notice, a refund. We never advertise to your list, and we do not sell it or share it. Everything we send carries a working unsubscribe, and anyone who opts out stays opted out.

06

Running the place

The parts that matter once you have been open a few months.

Numbers that answer a question

What sells, when you are busy, what a customer is worth, and how many visits become orders.

Including how people found you and where they gave up. Reckoned in your own timezone, so 'yesterday' means what you mean by yesterday.

Logins for your staff

Everyone gets their own, by invite.

A shared login means your order history cannot tell you who did what when something is disputed. Invites are single-use, and we never store a usable copy of one.

More than one location

Run several under one brand, switch between them, and report on one, some or all.

Each location keeps its own menu, hours and orders. You are not forced to run them as one restaurant or as strangers.

Help you can actually reach

Written answers, a ticket that goes to a person, and a call if it needs one.

The help center is open while you are still setting up, which is when people need it most. Tickets are read by a person, not a bot with a script.

We build your menu for you

Send us the menu you already have and we type it in, free. It is the one part of setup we do for you.

A photo of a printed menu, a PDF, a spreadsheet, or a link to your page on a delivery app is enough. Typing a menu in is the longest part of setting up and the part people give up on, so it is the part we do. Everything else is yours to do when it suits you: there is nothing to book and nobody to wait for.

Start to finish

What actually happens when someone orders.

In order, from them finding you to the money being in your bank.

  1. 1

    A customer finds you

    They search your name, tap Order Online on your Google listing, scan the QR on your counter, or follow the link in your bio. Your page opens - your logo, your menu, your prices.

  2. 2

    They order

    They pick their food, choose options, leave a note if they want, and give a name, a phone number and optionally an email. No account, no app.

  3. 3

    They pay

    By card at checkout, before the order ever reaches your board. The service fee is shown as its own line before they pay. Card money goes to your own account, not ours.

  4. 4

    You are told, within seconds

    The order appears on your board with a sound, lands in every inbox on your list, and prints in the kitchen if you have a printer. All three, not one of them.

  5. 5

    They can see where it is

    They get a tracking link with your address, your phone number and directions. Nobody has to stand at your counter asking.

  6. 6

    You cook it and mark it ready

    They are emailed the moment you do. If something is off, you can mark one item unavailable and refund just that line.

  7. 7

    They collect

    You mark it collected. If they never turn up, that is recorded too, with a time.

  8. 8

    You keep the customer

    Their name, number and what they ordered are on your list from that moment, and the list is yours to download whenever you want.

  9. 9

    The money reaches your bank

    On Stripe's payout schedule, from your own account. We never held it.

Straight about it

What it does not do.

Every platform has a gap. Ours is here rather than in the small print, because finding it after you have signed up is worse for both of us.

No delivery

This is pickup only. Customers collect from you. Plenty of restaurants run this alongside a delivery app and move their regulars over slowly. Delivery is being built, and it is in the list below rather than in this one, because it is not something you can use today.

Every order is paid by card, online

There is no pay-at-counter option and no cash on collection. The card is charged at checkout, before the ticket reaches your kitchen, which is also what pays for the software.

No table service

No table numbers, no ordering at the table, no splitting a bill between seats.

No reservations

We do not take bookings for your dining room. The only calendar here is for calls with us.

It does not replace your till

This sits beside whatever you already use for walk-ins. It does not run your front of house.

No text messages

Everything to your customers goes by email. There is no SMS.

No loyalty or points scheme

You get the customer list and can export it. There is no stamp card built in.

No stock, rotas or accounting

It counts orders and customers, not inventory or staff hours.

If you need one of these, tell us anyway. What owners ask for is how we decide what to build next, and we would rather hear it than have you sign up and find out. Send us a message.
